The Third Workshop on Ancient Language Processing (ALP 2027) is an official affiliated workshop of ACL 2027 and will take place alongside the main conference in Kyoto, Japan, in August 2027. This preliminary call invites submissions from researchers in computational linguistics, linguistics, ancient language studies, classical philology, history, philosophy, archaeology, digital humanities, and related fields.

Workshop Overview

Ancient languages preserve humanity’s earliest written records and intellectual and cultural achievements. They are also among the most challenging low-resource areas of natural language processing. ALP brings together computational research and traditional philology to develop methods and build a community around ancient languages and writing systems, from the emergence of writing around 3400 BCE to 800 CE.

Previous Workshops

  • ALP 2023 @ RANLP 2023 — Varna, Bulgaria. The workshop accepted 25 papers covering more than 14 ancient languages.
  • ALP 2025 @ NAACL 2025 — Albuquerque, USA. The workshop accepted 33 papers covering over ten languages, including Sumerian, Akkadian, Ancient Egyptian, and Classical Chinese.

Themes

We invite submissions addressing four core themes and welcome exploratory work on related topics:

  1. Evaluation under data scarcity: Design of benchmarks, metrics, and shared tasks for small, contested test sets built on scholarly text editions.
  2. Multimodal and visual processing of writing systems: OCR and handwritten text recognition for complex scripts, sign detection and segmentation, palaeographic analysis, and joint modelling of texts and their carrier artefacts.
  3. Large and small models in linguistic and philological workflows: Instruction-tuned and agent-based pipelines for lemmatization, text restoration, dating, and translation; comparison with task-specific small models; verifiability of generated outputs and provenance tracking.
  4. Data governance and reproducibility: Licensing frameworks for ancient language data, linked open data, lightweight computing, and data bias in cross-cultural research.

Related topics include character sets and Unicode standards, transliteration and input methods, tokenization, morphological, syntactic, and semantic analysis, machine translation, corpus construction, processing of fragmentary texts, and NLP applications in literary and historical studies.

Languages of interest

Languages of interest come from ancient civilizations worldwide, including but not limited to Sumerian, Akkadian, Elamite, Old Persian, Ancient Egyptian, Meroitic, Hittite, Ancient Greek, Latin, Sanskrit, Hebrew, Classical Chinese, Old Tibetan, Old Japanese, and Mayan.

Featured Shared Tasks

ALP 2027 will host two international shared evaluation tasks. Research teams are welcome to participate.

EvaCun 2027: OCR for Cuneiform Languages

The task asks teams to produce full digital transliterations from 2D images of cuneiform clay tablets. It offers two tracks: a vision-language model (VLM) only track and an open track that allows any OCR architecture. Evaluation measures recognition performance and segmentation quality overall and by attributes such as era. An extended evaluation uses a fixed machine translation model to assess downstream translation quality.

EvaHan 2027: Classical Chinese Text Difficulty Grading

This is the first international shared evaluation on Classical Chinese text difficulty grading. Systems classify Classical Chinese poetry and prose into ten difficulty levels, supporting reading adaptation and educational applications.

Submission Guidelines

Paper Types

  • Long papers (8 pages): Full original research presenting mature findings.
  • Short papers (4 pages): Innovative exploratory work, resource descriptions, and case studies.
  • Shared-task papers (4 pages): System descriptions from participants in EvaCun 2027 and/or EvaHan 2027.

All accepted papers will be published in the ACL Workshop Proceedings and indexed in the ACL Anthology.

Format Requirements

Submissions must use the official ACL LaTeX template and comply with the page limits, citation rules, and formatting requirements. Detailed specifications will be announced in the second call for papers.

Originality and Review

Submissions must present original, unpublished work and may not be submitted concurrently to other venues. All submissions will undergo double-blind peer review.

Diversity & Inclusion

The workshop welcomes contributions from researchers across computer-science groups, humanities departments, archaeological missions, museums and independent research teams worldwide. Our programme committee spans Europe, East Asia, North America and the Middle East, combining computational linguists and domain philologists. We encourage submissions from early-career researchers, graduate students, and scholars from communities traditionally under-represented in ACL-affiliated events. Shared-task participation is open to unaffiliated teams, and shared-task datasets are released under open licences.
